HD vs 4K IPTV Streaming: What You Really Need

Understand the difference between SD, HD, Full HD and 4K for IPTV, what your screen and connection need, and when higher quality is worth it.

Higher resolution looks sharper, but it also asks more from your connection, your device and your screen. It is not always the better choice.

What the terms mean

SD is the lowest common resolution. HD is usually 720p, and Full HD is 1080p. 4K, also called Ultra HD, has four times as many pixels as Full HD. More pixels need more data per second.

Is your screen able to show it?

A 4K stream on a Full HD television will not look sharper. Check your TV's specification before assuming you need 4K. Distance matters too: on a small screen across a room, the difference can be hard to see.

Does your connection allow it?

4K needs a much faster and steadier connection than HD, and live channels need stability more than any other type of video. If your connection is borderline, an HD stream that never stops is better than a 4K stream that pauses.

Device limits

Older streaming sticks may struggle with 4K decoding. Check that your device supports the format and that the app does too.

Frame rate matters for sport

Fast action such as football looks smoother at higher frame rates. A stable 1080p picture at a good frame rate often looks better in motion than a struggling 4K one. Prioritise smoothness for live sport.

HDR and other labels

HDR improves the range of light and dark in the picture but requires support on your TV and your device. Do not pay extra for a label your equipment cannot display.

Checking what you are actually getting

Look at the app's information panel during playback, if it has one, to see the resolution of the stream. Compare a few channels. Not every channel is available at the same quality.
